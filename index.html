01 / Meet the letters

Three shapes to remember.

Yud, vav, and heh are the three most frequent letters in a published Hebrew text sample. Their order varies by text. Start with their basic consonant sounds.

י

Yud / y

Like the y in yes.

Look for a tiny stroke. Yud is much shorter than vav.

ו

Vav / v

Like the v in van.

Look for a long vertical stroke with a small head.

ה

Heh / h

Like the h in hello.

A roof and two legs. The left leg does not touch the roof.

Letters can help with vowels, too. Yud often accompanies “ee.” Vav with a dot above, וֹ, represents “oh”; vav with a dot inside, וּ, represents “oo.” Final heh is often silent.
02 / Add a sound

Five vowel sounds. Little marks.

The dots and dashes are called niqqud. Read the consonant first, then its vowel. Here, heh carries one common mark for each sound.

Five sounds, more than five spellings: other vowel marks can make the same sounds. Most marks sit below the letter; holam sits above. Everyday Hebrew usually leaves niqqud out.

Try the sounds with yud and vav

יַ = ya · יֶ = yeh · יִ = yee · וַ = va · וֶ = veh · וִ = vee

Remember the special vowel spellings: וֹ = oh and וּ = oo.

03 / Your first word

Put it together.

הָיָה

ha-YA · “he was”

Start on the right: הָ = ha, then יָ = ya.
The final ה is silent. Stress the last syllable.

One extra mark: ָ is called kamatz. It usually makes the same “ah” sound as patach in Modern Hebrew.
